### Best Option: One-Shot (Show, Don't Tell)

This approach gives the model a perfect example to follow. Small models are excellent at pattern matching, making this the most reliable method.

```
Task: Reorder the sentences to create a logical story.

### Example
Sentences:
- He checked the time and saw he was late.
- The alarm clock rang loudly.
- He jumped out of bed.
- He quickly got dressed.

Correct Order:
["The alarm clock rang loudly.", "He jumped out of bed.", "He checked the time and saw he was late.", "He quickly got dressed."]

### Your Turn
Sentences:
{shuffled_sents}

Correct Order:
```

**Why it works best for Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct:**
*   **Simple Task Name:** "Reorder the sentences to create a logical story" is more intuitive than "based on their causal and temporal relations."
*   **Clear Structure:** The `###` separators clearly divide the instructions, the example, and the actual task.
*   **Concrete Example:** It *shows* the model exactly what to do and what the output format looks like (`["...", "...", "..."]`), which is far more effective than describing it in words.
*   **Implicit Logic:** The example implicitly teaches the model about "causal and temporal" order without using those complex terms.
*   **Direct Cue:** The final `Correct Order:` line directly prompts the model to generate the answer.

**Why it's better:**
*   **Strong Verb:** "Arrange" is a clear command.
*   **Simpler Goal:** "Coherent paragraph, ordered by time and logic" is easy to understand.
*   **Clear Formatting:** The instruction "Return your answer as a single Python list of strings" is unambiguous.
*   **Clean Separation:** Using a header like "Sentences to order:" helps the model distinguish the instructions from the data.

### Most Direct & Minimalist Option

If you need the absolute shortest prompt, this works by stripping away everything but the core command.

```
Sort these sentences into the correct logical order.

Sentences:
{shuffled_sents}

Sorted List:
```

**Why it's good:**
*   **Extremely Concise:** No wasted words.
*   **Clear Command:** "Sort these sentences" is a direct instruction.
*   **Simple Cue:** "Sorted List:" clearly indicates where the answer should go and hints at the format.
